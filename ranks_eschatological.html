السَّابِقُون
As-Sabiqoon — The Foremost
السابقون — آگے بڑھنے والے
Root: س — ب — ق · To precede · To race ahead · To arrive first · The Quran names them twice then calls them: Al-Muqarraboon
جڑ: س — ب — ق · پہلے پہنچنا · دوڑ کر آگے جانا
The Sabiqoon are the highest human station below Prophethood in the eschatological classification. Named twice without interrogation in Al-Waqiah — the rhetorical device the Quran reserves for the most important. Then immediately named: Al-Muqarraboon — the brought-near. Their station is proximity to Allah. Not paradise — proximity. The Quran says they will be "a few from the later generations" — a challenge written into revelation itself.
سابقون نبوت کے بعد سب سے اعلیٰ اخروی درجہ۔ الواقعہ میں دو بار بلا سوال نامزد — قرآن کا وہ اسلوب جو سب سے اہم کے لیے مخصوص ہے۔ پھر نام: المقربون۔ ان کا مقام اللہ سے قربت — جنت نہیں، قربت۔

S-B-Q in classical Arabic carries the image of a race — not a casual walk, but deliberate urgent forward movement toward a destination that matters. Sabaqa (he preceded) — Yasbiqu (he outpaces) — Sabiq (one who races ahead) — Istibaq (mutual racing, competing to arrive first). The root does not merely mean "first" — it implies the act of racing was chosen. A person chooses to be Sabiq. The Quran uses this root in 35:32 with bi-idhniLlah (by Allah's permission) to prevent pride: the racing is real and chosen, but the opening of the heart to race was given. Both are true simultaneously.
س-ب-ق عربی میں دوڑ کی تصویر — آہستہ چلنا نہیں، جان بوجھ کر اہم منزل کی طرف تیز حرکت۔ سبق — یسبق — سابق — استباق (ایک دوسرے سے آگے جانے کی کوشش)۔ جڑ صرف «پہلا» نہیں کہتی — یہ بتاتی ہے کہ دوڑ کا انتخاب کیا گیا۔ انسان سابق بننا چنتا ہے — لیکن دوڑنے کے لیے دل کا کھلنا اللہ کا عطیہ ہے۔

Middle Makki · Al-Waqiah 56 — The Primary Passage
اوسط مکی · الواقعہ ۵۶ — اصل مقام
Rev. #46 ترتیب #46 Al-Waqiah 56:10-12 وَالسَّابِقُونَ السَّابِقُونَ ۞ أُولَٰئِكَ الْمُقَرَّبُونَ ›
وَالسَّابِقُونَ السَّابِقُونَ ۞ أُولَٰئِكَ الْمُقَرَّبُونَ ۞ فِي جَنَّاتِ النَّعِيمِ
"And the Foremost — the Foremost. Those are the ones brought near — in Gardens of Delight."
«اور سابقون — سابقون۔ یہی مقرب ہیں — نعمتوں کے باغوں میں۔»
Surah Al-Waqiah 56:10-12
The Double Naming — Why It Matters
The Quran uses interrogative magnification for the other two groups: "The People of the Right — what are the People of the Right?" — emphasising their importance through a rhetorical question. But for the Sabiqoon: no question. Just the name twice. Was-Sabiqoona as-Sabiqoon. The repetition in classical Arabic grammar is called tawkid (emphasis, confirmation) — but here it also functions as identity. They are so defined by their racing that the word itself is their description. Then immediately: Ula'ika al-Muqarraboon — those are the brought-near. Not "those will enter paradise" but "those are brought near." Present tense. Ontological. This is their identity, not merely their reward.
دوسرے گروہوں کے لیے استفہام: «اصحاب الیمین — کیا ہیں اصحاب الیمین؟» لیکن سابقون کے لیے: کوئی سوال نہیں۔ صرف نام دو بار۔ پھر: المقربون — مضارع۔ یہ ان کی شناخت ہے، صرف انعام نہیں۔
Key Term
الْمُقَرَّبُون · Q-R-B · The brought-near (passive) — Allah brought them
Muqarraboon — passive participle of qarraba (to bring near). Allah brought them near — they did not achieve proximity through their own effort alone. The same root as Qurban (sacrifice — something offered to draw near), Qareeb (close), Qarib (to approach). The passive voice is the theological precision: the Sabiqoon raced (active) and were brought near (passive). Both the human action and the divine response are present in these two words.
مقربون — قرّب کا مفعول بہ۔ اللہ نے قریب کیا۔ قربان (قریب کرنے کی پیشکش) — قریب — قارب ایک جڑ سے۔ سابقون دوڑے (فاعل) اور قریب کیے گئے (مفعول)۔ دونوں ایک ساتھ۔
What This Passage Uniquely Establishes
The highest human station is not power, knowledge, piety, or worship — it is proximity. Muqarraboon. Being near to Allah. The entire taxonomy of the Quran has one ultimate measure: closeness to the One. Every other rank describes a quality or action. The Sabiqoon are described by where they end up: near. This reframes everything — the purpose of all spiritual development is not achievement but approach.
سب سے اعلیٰ مقام طاقت، علم، تقویٰ یا عبادت نہیں — قربت ہے۔ المقربون۔ قرآن کی پوری درجہ بندی کا ایک پیمانہ: ذاتِ واحد کے قریب ہونا۔

"A large group from the earlier generations — and a few from the later ones."
«اگلوں میں سے ایک بڑی جماعت — اور پچھلوں میں سے تھوڑے۔»
Surah Al-Waqiah 56:13-14
The Diminishing Sabiqoon — A Challenge Not a Destiny
The Quran explicitly states the Sabiqoon will be fewer in later generations. Thulla (a large group) from the Awwaleen — Qaleel (a few) from the Akhireen. This is one of the most sobering statements in the entire Quran for any believer living after the first generations. Ibn Kathir records that the Companions were disturbed by this when it was revealed — until the next passage clarified that the People of the Right from later generations would be in equal numbers to earlier ones (56:39-40). The Sabiqoon become rarer. The saved do not. The implication: the middle path remains accessible but the highest becomes increasingly rare — not because it is closed, but because fewer choose to race.
اگلوں میں ثلۃ — پچھلوں میں قلیل۔ ابن کثیر: صحابہ اس آیت سے پریشان ہوئے — یہاں تک کہ اگلی آیات نے واضح کیا اصحاب الیمین بعد والوں میں بھی برابر۔ سابقون نادر ہوتے ہیں — نجات نہیں۔
Key Term
قَلِيلٌ · Q-L-L · Few, small in number — deliberate Quranic word choice
Qaleel — few, small. Not none. The Quran does not say the Sabiqoon will not exist in later generations — it says they will be few. This is the challenge embedded in revelation: the path is open, the few who walk it are real and present in every generation, but they are few. Ibn al-Qayyim used this ayah to argue that the path of the Sabiqoon requires a deliberate counter-cultural choice — because culture, comfort, and social expectation all pull toward the Muqtasid middle, not the racing front.
قلیل — تھوڑے، کم تعداد میں۔ صفر نہیں۔ ہر زمانے میں سابقون موجود ہیں — لیکن کم۔ ابن القیم: سابقون کا راستہ جان بوجھ کر ثقافت کے خلاف انتخاب مانگتا ہے۔
What This Ayah Uniquely Demands
The Quran is telling every reader who lives in "later generations" that the Sabiqoon of their time will be few — and asking implicitly: will you be among them? This is not pessimism — it is the sharpest possible motivation. The rarity makes the choice more significant, not less achievable. It also explains why the path feels counter-cultural: it is supposed to. The Sabiqoon in any era are going against the grain of their time.
قرآن ہر بعد والے قاری سے پوچھ رہا ہے: کیا آپ اس تھوڑے میں ہوں گے؟ نادر پن راستے کو مشکل نہیں بناتا — انتخاب کو زیادہ اہم بناتا ہے۔

Middle Makki · Al-Fatir 35:32 — The Same Rank in This Life
اوسط مکی · فاطر ۳۵:۳۲ — اس زندگی میں یہی درجہ
Rev. #43 ترتیب #43 Al-Fatir 35:32 وَمِنْهُمْ سَابِقٌ بِالْخَيْرَاتِ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ ›
وَمِنْهُمْ سَابِقٌ بِالْخَيْرَاتِ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَضْلُ الْكَبِيرُ
"And among them is one who races ahead in good deeds by the permission of Allah — that is the great bounty."
«اور ان میں سے وہ ہے جو اللہ کے اذن سے نیکیوں میں آگے بڑھ گیا — یہی بڑا فضل ہے۔»
Surah Al-Fatir 35:32
Bi-idhniLlah — The Most Important Qualifier in This Verse
The Zalim li-nafsih and the Muqtasid have no qualifier attached — they operate by their own effort and default. The Sabiq bil-khayrat is specifically qualified: bi-idhniLlah — by Allah's permission. This is the Quran's antidote to the spiritual pride that would inevitably follow from being at the highest tier. The racing is real. The effort is real. The choice is real. But the opening of the heart that makes racing possible — that was given. Al-Ghazali uses this verse as the foundation for his entire teaching on tawfiq (divine facilitation): a person strives and Allah opens. The striving without the opening produces strain. The opening without striving produces nothing. Both are required — and the verse keeps the human action and the divine gift in precise balance.
ظالم اور مقتصد کے ساتھ کوئی قید نہیں — لیکن سابق کے ساتھ: باذن اللہ۔ روحانی غرور کا تریاق۔ دوڑ حقیقی، انتخاب حقیقی — لیکن دوڑنے کے لیے دل کا کھلنا عطاء تھا۔ الغزالی: یہ آیت توفیق کی پوری تعلیم کی بنیاد ہے۔
The Description That Completes Al-Waqiah
Al-Waqiah tells us where the Sabiqoon end up (Al-Muqarraboon — proximity). Al-Fatir tells us what they look like in this life (racing in good deeds, sustained, by Allah's grace). Together: the Sabiq in this world is the Muqarrab in the next. The station of the next life is the continuation of the state of this life. This makes the Sabiq's path visible — it is not a mystical unreachable destination. It is the person who, right now, races rather than walks, sustains rather than drifts, and attributes his ability to race to Allah rather than to himself.
الواقعہ بتاتی ہے سابقون کہاں پہنچتے ہیں (مقربون)۔ فاطر بتاتی ہے وہ اس زندگی میں کیسے لگتے ہیں (نیکیوں میں دوڑنا، مستقل، اللہ کے فضل سے)۔ اگلی دنیا کا مقام اس دنیا کی کیفیت کا تسلسل ہے۔

"And race toward forgiveness from your Lord and a Garden whose width is the heavens and the earth — prepared for the Muttaqeen."
«اور اپنے رب کی مغفرت اور اس جنت کی طرف دوڑو جس کی چوڑائی آسمانوں اور زمین جتنی ہے — متقین کے لیے تیار ہے۔»
Surah Al-Imran 3:133
The Command Form — Saari'oo
Saari'oo — imperative plural of saaara (to race, to make haste). The same verb used for the running between Safa and Marwa in Hajj. The Quran is not suggesting or recommending — it is commanding. Race. The destination: first, maghfira (forgiveness — the clearing of the past) then jannah. Notice the sequence: forgiveness before paradise. The Sabiq does not race toward reward — he races toward the clearing of what stands between him and Allah. The reward follows, but it is not the goal. This is the inner orientation that distinguishes the Sabiq from the person who performs the same acts for a different reason.
سارعوا — فعل امر۔ صفا مروہ کی سعی والا فعل۔ منزل: پہلے مغفرت، پھر جنت۔ ترتیب اہم: سابق اجر کی طرف نہیں دوڑتا — اللہ اور اس کے درمیان حائل چیز دور کرنے کی طرف دوڑتا ہے۔

ق
Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyyah (d. 751 AH)
Madarij al-Salikin · Al-Wabil al-Sayyib · The Station Analysis
مدارج السالکین · الوابل الصیب · درجات کا تجزیہ
›
"The Sabiq is not one who simply performs the obligatory and adds the voluntary — this describes the Muqtasid at best. The Sabiq is one whose inward state is continuously operating even when all outward acts have been completed. His dhikr does not stop when his prayer ends. His muraqabah does not cease when he is in the marketplace. His love of Allah operates as a background condition of every moment, not a state he enters for specific acts. The racing is first of the heart, then of the limbs."
«سابق وہ نہیں جو صرف فرض ادا کرے اور نوافل جوڑے — یہ زیادہ سے زیادہ مقتصد ہے۔ سابق وہ ہے جس کی باطنی کیفیت تمام ظاہری اعمال کے بعد بھی مسلسل کام کرتی رہے۔ اس کا ذکر نماز کے بعد نہیں رکتا۔ بازار میں مراقبہ ختم نہیں ہوتا۔ اللہ کی محبت ہر لمحے کی پس منظر شرط ہے۔ دوڑ پہلے دل کی، پھر اعضاء کی۔»
Ibn al-Qayyim's most important contribution to understanding the Sabiqoon: he shifts the definition from outward quantity of acts to inward continuity of state. The Sabiq and Muqtasid may perform identical outward acts — same prayers, same fasts, same charity. What differs is the inward operating system: the Muqtasid's inner state is intermittent; the Sabiq's is continuous. This is why the Sabiq's acts produce transformative effects while the identical acts of the Muqtasid produce reward without the same inner transformation. Ibn al-Qayyim traces this through Madarij al-Salikin in the stations of Yaqazah (wakefulness), Tawbah, Muhasabah, Inabah, and eventually Mahabbah — the Sabiq is the one who has passed through all of these into a state where the inner machinery operates without constant effort.
ابن القیم کا سب سے اہم کردار: وہ تعریف کو ظاہری مقدار سے باطنی تسلسل کی طرف منتقل کرتے ہیں۔ سابق اور مقتصد ایک جیسے ظاہری اعمال کر سکتے ہیں — فرق باطنی نظام میں ہے: مقتصد کی کیفیت متقطع، سابق کی مسلسل۔
Madarij al-Salikin, Vol. 1 — Station of Yaqazah through Mahabbah · Al-Wabil al-Sayyib, Chapter on Dhikr's effects on the heart
غ
Abu Hamid al-Ghazali (d. 505 AH)
Ihya Ulum al-Din · The Systematic Framework
احیاء علوم الدین · منظم فریم ورک
›
"Know that people are of three kinds in relation to the Next World: the heedless who are occupied with this world entirely; the one who knows the Next World but whose nafs pulls him toward this world — he fights himself constantly; and the one who has broken the nafs's pull entirely so that his heart is at rest in the remembrance of Allah and his limbs race toward good deeds the way a hungry man races toward food. This third is the Sabiq, and his racing is no longer effort — it is nature."
«جانو کہ لوگ آخرت کے لحاظ سے تین قسم: دنیا میں غرق غافل؛ آخرت جاننے والا لیکن نفس اسے دنیا کی طرف کھینچتا — مسلسل لڑتا ہے؛ اور وہ جس نے نفس کی کشش بالکل توڑ دی — دل اللہ کے ذکر میں مطمئن، اعضاء نیکی کی طرف دوڑتے ہیں جیسے بھوکا کھانے کی طرف دوڑتا ہے۔ یہ تیسرا سابق ہے، اور اس کی دوڑ اب محنت نہیں — فطرت ہے۔»
Al-Ghazali's key insight is that the Sabiq's racing is not willpower — it has become second nature. He draws a sharp distinction between the second category (the fighting believer, the Muqtasid) and the third (the Sabiq): both perform the same acts, but the second performs them against resistance while the third performs them with a momentum that has replaced resistance with ease. Al-Ghazali traces this transformation specifically to the purification of the nafs through sustained practice of mujahadah (self-striving), until the nafs no longer resists and the heart's natural orientation toward Allah becomes the dominant operating state. His framework is trainable — it is a progression, not a gift.
الغزالی کی اہم بصیرت: سابق کی دوڑ قوتِ ارادی نہیں — دوسری فطرت بن گئی ہے۔ دوسری اور تیسری قسم میں فرق: دونوں ایک جیسے اعمال — لیکن دوسرا مزاحمت کے ساتھ، تیسرا رفتار کے ساتھ۔ یہ مجاہدے سے حاصل ہوتا ہے۔
Ihya Ulum al-Din, Book 40 — Dhikr al-Mawt; Book 31 — Tawbah; Book 36 — Mahabbah

Al-Muhasibi's contribution is the most psychologically uncomfortable: he questions the motivation of the racing itself. His entire framework in Al-Ri'ayah is built on the detection of riya (ostentation) — the spiritual counterfeiter inside every person. His point about the Sabiqoon is sharp: the nafs can simulate racing for the reward of being known as a Sabiq. The true test is hiddenness — does your racing continue when it produces no social benefit, no reputation, no recognition? The Sabiq races in the unseen. The person imitating the Sabiq needs an audience. This is the Muhasibi test that no outward observable measure can determine.

Ibn al-Qayyim and Al-Ghazali both emphasise the trainable, achievable, effortful path to becoming Sabiq — the stations, the mujahadah, the sustained practice. The Placement of Siddiqoon — Second After Prophethood
The order in this ayah is not casual — it is a ranked sequence. Nabiyyoon (Prophets) — then Siddiqoon — then Shuhada — then Salihoon. The Siddiqoon are placed above the Shuhada and Salihoon. This is the Quran's direct confirmation that the station of complete truthfulness is higher than martyrdom in the path of Allah. This seems counterintuitive until you understand what Siddiqiyyah actually requires: complete inner-outer alignment in every dimension of life, sustained over a lifetime, with no gap ever. Martyrdom is a single moment of ultimate truth. Siddiqiyyah is every moment of every day, without exception.

The Siddiq's Defining Quality
لَا فَرْق · No gap · Between what is believed, said, and done
The classical scholars define Siddiqiyyah as the complete absence of any gap between: (1) what is believed in the heart; (2) what is declared with the tongue; (3) what is done with the limbs; (4) what is shown in public; (5) what is done in private. Any single gap — between belief and speech, between speech and action, between public and private — removes the person from the station of Siddiq. This is why the title is rare and why Abu Bakr RA received it: not because he performed the most acts, but because he had no gap in any dimension at any time.

The Definition of Siddiqoon in This Ayah
This is the most direct Quranic definition of who the Siddiqoon are: those who believe in Allah and His messengers. The Shuhada are described here as having their reward AND their light (wa-nooruhum). The same light (noor) that appears on the Day of Judgment for the believers in 57:12 — running before them. The Siddiqoon are identified by their iman; the Shuhada by their light. Both stations arrive at the same divine mercy but from different paths. The Siddiq arrives through the completeness of inner-outer truth. The Shahid arrives through the ultimate testimony of sacrifice.

The Complete Station Analysis · The Five Dimensions of Sidq
مکمل درجہ تجزیہ · صدق کے پانچ پہلو
›
"Sidq has five dimensions that must all be present simultaneously for a person to be called Siddiq: Sidq al-Niyyah (truthfulness of intention — the act is done purely for Allah); Sidq al-Azm (truthfulness of resolve — when you decide to do something good, you actually do it); Sidq al-Wafa (truthfulness of fulfilling — you fulfil what you promised, including what you promised to Allah); Sidq al-'Amal (truthfulness of action — the outward act matches the inward state); and Sidq fi Maqamat al-Deen (truthfulness in the stations — when you claim to have fear, you actually have fear; when you claim love, it is real love). The Siddiq has no gap in any of these five. That is what 'something settled in the chest' means."
«صدق کے پانچ پہلو بیک وقت موجود ہونے چاہئیں: نیت میں صدق؛ عزم میں صدق؛ وفا میں صدق؛ عمل میں صدق؛ دین کے مقامات میں صدق۔ صدیق کے پاس ان پانچوں میں سے کوئی فرق نہیں۔ یہی «سینے میں جمی چیز» ہے۔»
Ibn al-Qayyim's five-dimensional framework is the most analytically rigorous treatment of Siddiqiyyah in the classical literature. Each dimension represents a different potential gap: most people have sidq in one or two dimensions but gaps in the others. A person who has truthful intention but weak resolve (starts many good acts and completes few) fails at Sidq al-Azm. A person who fulfils promises to others but not to Allah fails at Sidq al-Wafa. A person whose outward piety is greater than their inward state fails at Sidq al-'Amal. The Siddiq has closed all five gaps — not temporarily, but as a constitutive state.

The Living Dead — What Shahada Does to Death
The Quran makes an extraordinary ontological claim: the Shuhada are not dead. They are alive. The verse uses the emphatic negative La Tahsabanna (do not at all consider) — indicating that the opposite assumption is the natural one, the one that needs to be corrected. The correction is fundamental: death in the path of Allah is not death. It is a transition into a state of being "with their Lord" (inda rabbihim) — the same phrase used for the Muqarraboon. Fariheena (rejoicing) in the present participle — it is their ongoing state, not a one-time event. They are alive, provided for, and rejoicing right now.

◈ The Analytical Core — What Unifies All Types of Shahid
◈ تجزیاتی مرکز — کیا چیز تمام شہداء کو جوڑتی ہے
The classical scholars — especially Ibn al-Qayyim in Zad al-Ma'ad — noted that what unifies all the types of Shahid is not the manner of death but the quality of the testimony. Every type of Shahid died while engaged in something that constitutes testimony to a truth greater than the self: the plague victim who accepted his condition as Allah's decree; the drowning one; the one defending his family. What makes the battlefield Shahid the paradigm is not that he died violently but that the entirety of his final act was testimony — his presence on the battlefield was already an act of bearing witness to the supremacy of Allah over the fear of death. The living Shahid — every person whose life is a continuous act of testimony — is understood in this tradition as sharing in the spiritual reality of this station without the physical death.

Ibrahim AS — the Friend of Allah (Khalilullah), the one who passed every test, whose millah (way) the Quran calls us to follow, the father of the prophets — makes a prayer. The Three-Part Prayer — Its Sequence
Ibrahim AS asks for three things in sequence: Hukm (wisdom, judgment — the ability to see and decide correctly) → joining with the Saliheen → a tongue of truth that will endure. This sequence is architectural: wisdom produces the ability to act rightly, which makes one Salih, which produces lasting truthful speech. The Quran references a written decree — in the Zabur (Psalms of Dawud AS) and confirmed again after the Dhikr (the original heavenly scripture) — that the earth's inheritance belongs to the Saliheen. The Middle Position — What It Actually Contains
The Muqtasid is between the Zalim li-nafsih and the Sabiq bil-khayrat. Classical scholars differ on whether the verse is listed in ascending or descending order — Ibn Kathir and most say ascending (Zalim → Muqtasid → Sabiq). Some note that the Zalim is mentioned first as a mercy — even the one who wrongs himself is among the chosen. The Muqtasid fulfils: all five pillars performed correctly, the obligatory fasts and zakah and hajj if capable, avoidance of the clearly prohibited, basic honesty and justice in dealings. He does not consistently add nawafil, does not have a sustained dhikr practice beyond what is prescribed, and his inner life is functional but not continuously operating. Al-Qurtubi: this is the majority of the saved.

What Adam AS's Tawbah Reveals About This Station
Adam AS is a prophet — the first human — and his first documented act after the error is to say: Rabbana zalamna anfusana (Our Lord — we have wronged ourselves). This is the defining characteristic of the Zalim li-nafsih who will eventually be saved: they acknowledge the zulm. They do not blame the circumstances (Iblis made me do it), they do not minimise it, they do not delay the acknowledgment. "We wronged ourselves" — first person plural, immediate, direct, to Allah. The contrast with Iblis is deliberate: Iblis blamed Allah ("because You misled me"). Adam AS blamed himself. This difference is the difference between a person who remains in the Zalim station and one who moves through it and beyond.
آدم علیہ السلام نبی تھے — اور غلطی کے بعد ان کا پہلا عمل: ربنا ظلمنا انفسنا۔ ظالم لنفسہ جو بچتا ہے وہ ظلم تسلیم کرتا ہے۔ ابلیس سے فرق: ابلیس نے اللہ کو الزام دیا۔ آدم علیہ السلام نے خود کو۔ یہی فرق ظالم رہنے اور آگے بڑھنے کا فرق ہے۔
The Escape — Tawbah as the Exit from Zulm

Adam AS's prayer is theologically precise: "If You do not forgive AND have mercy." Two separate divine acts — maghfira (forgiveness — the covering/removal of the sin) and rahma (mercy — the positive inflow of grace). The Zalim li-nafsih needs both: the removal of the sin AND the positive restoration. He cannot restore himself through his own effort. This acknowledgment of complete dependence is itself part of tawbah — the recognition that the exit from the Zalim station requires divine action, not merely human effort. The human contribution is the acknowledgment and the return. The divine contribution is the forgiveness and the mercy.

The fact that the Quran records a Prophet — the father of humanity — using the phrase "zalamna anfusana" is the most important thing about this rank. It means: this station is not a marker of being outside the saved community. It is a transient state that even the greatest souls have passed through. The permanent Zalim li-nafsih is the one who refuses to acknowledge the zulm. The transient Zalim — Adam AS's type — acknowledges, seeks forgiveness, and moves. Every person on the spiritual path has been in this station. The question is whether they acknowledged it or stayed in it.
